# Arena CBT — Phase 2.1 Business Rules Closure

**Status:** decision register for school-owner and technical review. No option below is an approved MAN 1 Palembang policy unless explicitly marked “confirmed foundation.” Recommendations are technical advice only. This document does not authorize schema freeze, migrations, or implementation.

Related documents: [Phase 2 Design Review Revision 2](./phase-2-design-rev2.md), [Revision 2 ERD](./erd-rev2.mmd), [Revision 1 design](./phase-2-design.md), [architecture proposal](./architecture-proposal.md).

## Findings summary

Revision 2 closes much of the technical shape, but it still contains proposals that affect student eligibility, attempt/result history, deadline outcomes, grading, role authority, identity recovery, and retention. Those are not safely inferable from repository code. They need a named school owner to decide before the corresponding schema/API contract is frozen.

There are also implementation choices that do not set school policy: transaction/locking strategy, idempotency, provider pagination validation, bounded pools, and staging checks. The technical team may recommend these, but must verify compatibility and resource assumptions in an isolated staging target before relying on them.

No evidence in the reviewed documents establishes production MySQL version, server headroom, a 1,200-student capacity result, school retention periods, or the school’s grading/discipline rules. These remain explicitly unverified.

## Critical decisions to resolve first

These are the minimum school decisions that block schema/API freeze for the related features:

1. **Who is eligible, and when is that list fixed?** Proposal: freeze roster and class/semester context when the assignment is published; require an active account at start. Decide how to correct a roster after publication.
2. **What extra attempts are allowed and who can authorize them?** Decide whether susulan, remedial, and retake are distinct, whether a parent attempt is required, maximum count, validity window, and whether cancelled/expired attempts consume a slot.
3. **What happens at the deadline and when an assignment is paused or closed?** Choose the authoritative cutoff rule and grace/in-flight treatment. Decide whether running attempts continue or are terminated.
4. **How are work and results graded and which attempt counts?** Decide first-release question types, partial/negative scoring, blank answers, rounding/caps, result-selection policy, tie-break, and treatment of expired/terminated work.
5. **Who can terminate, grade, publish, reset PINs, and resolve identity conflicts?** Approve role boundaries and named operational responsibility.
6. **How long must records be retained?** School/records owner must set periods for answers, mutation receipts, results/revisions, audit, sync issues, sessions, and academic snapshots.

The remaining details and impacts are in the matrices below. “Blocks schema freeze” means that a field, constraint, endpoint contract, or state transition cannot safely be finalized until the decision is made. “Can decide later” means the proposed model can preserve options, but the behavior must be approved before the affected feature is exposed. “Staging verification” means verify against an isolated non-production target; it does not authorize production access or changes.

## Technical decisions the team may own

These are engineering mechanisms, not school rules. The technical lead may finalize them in the design after review; changes that alter student-facing policy still return to the school owner.

## Cross-document audit and assumed items

The following Revision 2 statements look concrete but remain proposals or technical constraints rather than approved school policy:

- Eligibility snapshot at assignment publication and active-account recheck at start.
- `PRIMARY_ONLY`, `LATEST_APPROVED`, and `HIGHEST_APPROVED` are candidate result policies; no default is adopted here.
- Makeup/remedial/retake category semantics and parent-attempt requirements.
- Assignment pause/close behavior for already-started attempts.
- DB-lock-time deadline adjudication versus trusted ingress timestamp/grace.
- Whether expired, terminated, or cancelled attempts are graded or eligible for selection.
- Question type launch scope; scoring formula, blank handling, partial/negative credit, rounding, cap, and tie-break.
- Teacher/invigilator/admin scopes and local admin identity.
- PIN issuance/reset and NISN collision/rebind process.
- Student session duration/device policy and all retention periods.
- Enrollment history completeness and deactivation/reconciliation authority.
- MySQL 8.4/InnoDB baseline, pool/worker starting values, provisional SLOs, and load profile.
- `arena_cbt_next` is only a placeholder database name; the real isolated DB name/availability is not confirmed.
- Systemd is a deployment proposal, not a verified host requirement.
- Existing “three violations” behavior must not be carried forward without school approval.

The following are **confirmed project foundations**, carried forward from the requester’s prior decisions: Fastify, strict TypeScript, React, Vite, MySQL family, modular monolith; a database isolated from legacy Arena; Portal Data as initial academic source behind a replaceable adapter (not assumed to be SIPADU itself); student NISN + local PIN stored only as a secure hash; teacher/employee OIDC through Portal Data; preserve all attempt/answer/result history; server is authoritative for exam timing, accepted answers, submission, and grading; no unverified 1,200-user claim.

The verified Portal Data route/pagination details in Revision 2 are repository evidence. They do not by themselves prove that a complete snapshot is authoritative for deactivation, that enrollment history exists, or that upstream SIPADU has the same contract.
